Verdict

For: Market leader (41% share, ~70% in the EV segment) in a component whose value per vehicle rises 1.5–2.5x as two-wheelers electrify, priced at 44.9x — a genuine discount to the ~52–58x peer-set average.

Against: Working capital days ballooned from 12 to 102 even as margins compressed (EBITDA 18.3%→15.7%), and the co-promoter (Bain Capital) only bought in ~16 months before this listing with no non-compete — this isn't a decades-long founder-only story being rewarded, it's a recent financial-sponsor exit dressed as a growth IPO.

Core strengths vs weaknesses

Strengths

  • Category leadership with real switching costs. #1/2 in 2W/3W wiring harness (41% share) and clear leader in the EV sub-segment (~70%), built on custom tooling per OEM platform that makes supplier switching expensive.
  • EV content tailwind, no new customers needed. An EV harness carries 1.5–2.5x the value of an ICE one; EV revenue already grew from 16.2% to 24.2% of sales in two years without Dhoot having to win a single new account.
  • Best-in-class margin among listed harness peers. FY26 adjusted EBITDA margin of 15.71% beats Minda Corporation (11.66%) and Motherson Sumi Wiring (9.24%).
  • Priced at a discount to the broader peer set. 44.9x post-issue P/E against a peer-set average commonly cited between 52x–58x.
  • Balance sheet actively de-risking. D/E improved from 0.78 to 0.35 in a year, and over half the fresh issue goes straight to further debt paydown at the company and its subsidiaries.

Weaknesses

  • Cash conversion is deteriorating even as margins compress. Net working-capital days jumped from 12 to 102, and adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 18.31% to 15.71% in two years — growth is costing more cash and more margin at the same time.
  • Heavy customer and plant concentration. Top 5 customers = 71.56% of FY26 revenue (up from 66.17% in FY24); one manufacturing site alone accounts for 50.5% of wiring-harness capacity. No long-term volume contracts back any of it.
  • The "promoter" only bought in 16 months ago. Bain Capital's BC Asia Investments XV acquired its ~49–55% stake around April 2025 (CCI-approved March 2025); it has explicitly disclosed it lacks operating experience in the business and carries no non-compete with the company.
  • Nearly a quarter of the fresh issue has no named use. After debt repayment (₹766.6 cr) and the two new plants (₹150 cr), roughly ₹483 cr — about 34% of the fresh issue — is earmarked for unidentified acquisitions and general corporate purposes.
  • Related-party dealings and a disclosed cyberattack. A FY24 sale of ~₹81 cr of assets to a subsidiary, a recurring "business strategy" fee to Bain Capital, and a cybersecurity incident in FY25 are all called out in the RHP's own risk factors.

Not found / not disclosed. The RHP restates only FY24–FY26 (SEBI's standard three-year requirement); no pre-FY24 RoC filings or prior DRHP figures for Dhoot Transmission surfaced in the sources checked. The FY24 starting point is itself a high base (already profitable, already growing), so the ~27% revenue CAGR shown above is not inflated by a low-base effect the way some IPO CAGRs are.

Cash flow — does the profit turn into cash?

Operating cash flow is reported as positive and growing in all three disclosed years — the first cash-flow test (does profit convert to cash) is passed at a qualitative level. But the exact CFO ₹ figures per year, and therefore CFO/PAT and CFO/EBITDA ratios, were not found in the secondary sources checked — verify against the RHP's restated cash flow statement before relying on this. The second test (does the business fund its own growth) is harder to read cleanly: net working-capital days worsened sharply from 12 to 102 over the disclosed period, which by itself consumes cash even while the P&L shows growing profit — a classic case where the two cash-flow questions can point in different directions. One source citing DRHP-stage numbers shows a FY26 net cash flow of ₹998.30 cr driven by a financing inflow of ₹1,912.33 cr — almost certainly reflecting Bain Capital's equity infusion into the company rather than organic operating cash generation, so it should not be read as an operating cash-flow strength.

Forward outlook & stated plans

The RHP commits ₹150 cr (of a ₹226.34 cr total project cost, balance from internal accruals) to two new wiring-harness plants at Jhajjar (Haryana) and Hosur (Tamil Nadu), adding a stated 30.6 lakh units of annual capacity. No brokerage-published forward EPS estimate was found in the sources checked, other than one independent analyst's own back-of-envelope FY27E EPS of ~₹25 (SPTulsian) — at a trailing 44.9x multiple, the absence of a house forward-EPS view from any of the subscribing brokerages is itself worth noting: several of the "Subscribe" calls are pricing the story on trailing FY26 earnings rather than a published forward number.

Purpose of the issue

Use of proceeds (of the ₹1,400 cr fresh issue)

Commentary: This is not a pure-OFS red flag — 45.6% of the issue is fresh capital, above the 25% floor. But look at what the fresh capital actually does: ~55% of it (₹766.57 cr) repays debt at the company and its subsidiaries, only ~11% funds the two named capacity additions, and roughly a third (~34%) is earmarked for unidentified acquisitions and general corporate purposes — a blank cheque by definition. Combined with the 54.4% OFS, where BC Asia Investments XV (Bain Capital) and Mangalam Capital sell down, a meaningful share of what investors are paying for is Bain partially cashing out a ~16-month-old stake and the company deleveraging, not new growth capital being deployed into the business you'd be buying into.

Promoter & lifeline

Rahul Radhavallabh Dhoot & BC Asia Investments XV Limited (Bain Capital) — Rahul Dhoot (b. 5 Mar 1973, age 53) holds a bachelor's in electronics & telecommunication engineering from Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Marathwada University, Aurangabad, and has been with the company since 28 April 1998 — 27+ years in the automotive sector, all at Dhoot. BC Asia Investments XV Limited is a Bain Capital investment vehicle that acquired its ~49–55% stake around April 2025 (CCI approval March 2025); the RHP itself discloses that BC Asia XV "does not possess adequate experience in the Company's line of business" and carries no non-compete with the company or its other directors.

Personal history shown from Rahul Dhoot's disclosed year of birth; education year and any career milestones before joining the company in 1998 were not disclosed in the RHP excerpts reviewed. BC Asia Investments XV Limited was itself only incorporated on 13 March 2023.

Apply for listing gains

If GMP holds near current levels (~28%) into allotment and you're comfortable with a lottery on ~3x retail subscription — the anchor book and NII demand suggest reasonable Day-1 pop odds, but this is a bet on sentiment holding for five more days, not on the business.

Apply and hold

If you believe the EV-content tailwind (1.5–2.5x harness value per EV) plays out over 3+ years and you can look past a 16-month-old PE promoter and a still-unidentified ~34% of the fresh issue — the post-issue balance sheet is genuinely cleaner, and the discount to the broad peer set is real.

Skip

If you weigh the co-promoter's short tenure and the OFS-heavy structure as more important than the EV story, or if you specifically want Motherson Sumi Wiring's cleaner balance sheet and higher RoNW at a near-identical multiple instead.

Who this is for

Suits you if

  • You want direct exposure to India's 2W/3W EV-content growth story through the segment leader
  • You can tolerate a High-risk, concentrated small-cap position sized at a single retail lot
  • You're comfortable holding through a co-promoter's first 16 months of ownership without a long track record to judge them by

Does not suit you if

  • You need low customer/plant concentration or a clean, uncomplicated promoter history
  • You'd rather pay a similar multiple for Motherson Sumi Wiring's stronger balance sheet and RoNW
  • You're not prepared to sit through the Feb 2027 / Feb 2028 lock-in unlocks as a small-float stock
Summary: The single figure that most supports applying is the 44.9x post-issue P/E against a peer-set average commonly cited near 55x — a real, quantifiable discount for the category leader in a segment with a structural EV tailwind. The single figure that most argues against it is that ~34% of the fresh issue (roughly ₹483 cr) has no named use, layered on top of a co-promoter who has held the stake for barely 16 months and takes a disclosed annual fee from the company — which is a very different investment case from "buy the market leader's growth capital" even though the headline multiple looks similar either way. Both are true at the same time, and the ₹14,807 stake you've set for this one won't turn on resolving that tension either way.
